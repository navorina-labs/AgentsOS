# Marketing operating system

Use this reference for positioning, planning, channels, campaigns, launches, and conversion work.

## Diagnose before choosing tactics

Model growth as connected transitions:

```text
qualified audience -> relevant visit -> meaningful activation -> paid value -> retained value -> advocacy
```

Find the weakest transition using first-party evidence. A high visit count cannot compensate for weak relevance, activation, or retention.

## ICP contract

Define an ICP with observable qualifiers:

- situation and trigger;
- job to be done and costly consequence;
- current alternative;
- authority, budget, urgency, and implementation constraints;
- reachable communities or search behavior;
- disqualifiers.

Avoid demographic decoration that does not change the offer, channel, or message.

## Positioning contract

Write one sentence for each field:

1. **For:** narrow customer in a recognizable situation.
2. **Problem:** important job or friction with an existing alternative.
3. **Category:** understandable frame of reference.
4. **Outcome:** customer value, not a feature list.
5. **Difference:** mechanism or capability competitors cannot trivially claim.
6. **Proof:** product evidence, customer evidence, or demonstrable artifact.

If proof is missing, convert the claim into a hypothesis and create a proof-building task.

## Message hierarchy

Order the message around the buying decision:

1. problem and desired outcome;
2. who the product is for;
3. differentiated mechanism;
4. concrete capabilities;
5. proof and risk reduction;
6. objections and boundaries;
7. next action.

Each page or campaign should have one dominant conversion objective. Supporting actions may reduce commitment, but must not compete visually or semantically with the primary action.

## Channel selection

Score channels against:

- audience concentration;
- intent strength;
- ability to demonstrate the product;
- feedback speed;
- cost and operational capacity;
- compounding value;
- policy and brand risk.

Select one primary motion and one supporting motion until evidence justifies expansion. Channel breadth is not strategy.

## Content portfolio

Assign every asset a job:

- `discover`: satisfy a real problem or question and earn qualified attention;
- `evaluate`: compare approaches, explain tradeoffs, or demonstrate expertise;
- `convert`: resolve objections and make the next action clear;
- `activate`: help a new user reach first value;
- `retain`: deepen use, recover failure, or reveal new value;
- `prove`: publish original data, benchmarks, case studies, or product evidence.

Prefer original evidence and product-linked utility over commodity summaries. Refresh or retire assets when intent, product behavior, or source guidance changes.

## Experiment specification

For each experiment record:

- observed problem;
- hypothesis and mechanism;
- audience and funnel transition;
- treatment and control or comparison baseline;
- primary metric and guardrail metric;
- minimum runtime or sample rationale;
- rejection condition;
- decision for positive, neutral, and negative results;
- owner and implementation cost.

Do not call an unmeasured tactic an experiment.
